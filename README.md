## The entry

> A traffic jam is a herd of elephants all trying to squeeze through one garden gate at once, so the whole herd shuffles forward one tiny step at a time.
>
> It works because the elephants show a crowd of big, slow things stuck at one narrow opening, but it leaves out that cars are driven by people making choices, not animals following a herd.

Line 1 is a single vivid sentence built on one analogy: a traffic jam as a herd
of elephants at one garden gate. Line 2 says in one sentence what the analogy
maps — a crowd of big, slow things stuck at one narrow opening — and what it leaves
out: cars are driven by people making choices, not animals following a herd.

## Build notes

- **Why a traffic jam, why elephants:** the crowded motifs in this contest are
  the toaster as a sunbed or lift, rain as the sky wringing out a sponge, and a
  queue as a train or caterpillar. A herd of elephants at a single garden gate
  is a fresh picture a 10-year-old can see at once, and the narrow gate is
  exactly what makes a jam a jam: too much trying to pass through too little.
- **One analogy only:** line 1 carries a single comparison from start to
  finish — no list of metaphors, no second picture crowding it.
- **Honest second line:** line 2 names the mapping (a crowd of big, slow
  things, one narrow opening) and the limit (drivers choose; a herd follows)
  instead of praising the analogy.
- **Gate:** the output is 2 lines, 340 characters including the trailing
  newline, one sentence per line, UTF-8, inside the 1–400 character gate.

## How it runs

`main.py` is deterministic, standard-library only, and uses no network. It
writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt`:

```sh
python3 main.py
```
